Model overview

Four solution families, one central axis.

Nexus, Orbit, Vista and Add-ons are the four solution families of NOVA. Orbit and Vista form the central axis and the first delivery focus; Nexus supports development and operations; Add-ons extends the model with complementary directions.

Central axis

Orbit–Vista: connect, coordinate and work.

Orbit and Vista form the central axis: Orbit connects and coordinates business capabilities; Vista brings them to users in one consistent workspace. This is NOVA’s first delivery focus.

01

Connect existing business capabilities

Orbit works with existing programs and data, opening modern ways to use them with limited deep changes to the core.

02

Coordinate business flows

The processing steps of a business function are coordinated in one flow, with time and load controls.

03

Report unclear transaction outcomes

When an outcome is unclear, the system reports it so operations can reconcile instead of resending the transaction automatically.

04

One consistent workspace

Vista brings menus, forms, data tables and actions into the same way of working across business functions.

Four solution families

One role per family in the model.

All four solution families belong to the NOVA model; Orbit and Vista form the central axis and the first delivery focus.

01

Nexus — development and operations tools

The development and operations family: build, environments, test data and shared administration. Its intended scope extends to DevOps, batch and daily operational tasks.

02

Orbit — connect and coordinate

Connects business capabilities from existing systems, coordinates processing flows and opens modern ways to use them with limited deep changes to the core. The Aster demonstration software ships with the Orbit family so a flow can be experienced without using a running system.

03

Vista — one consistent workspace

Menus, tabs, forms, data tables and actions follow the same way of working, so users can move between business functions without relearning the interface.

04

Add-ons — complementary capabilities

Complementary directions around the Orbit–Vista axis, including AIRA, AIRAS and the long-term direction of Astra — a next-generation insurance core. This is a direction of the model.

Deployment questions

What needs to be clear before deployment.

Do we need to replace the entire core at the outset?

Not necessarily. You can begin by letting Orbit work with existing programs and data after checking compatibility. The direction is to expand services, then transition individual business functions when ready; each change needs its own scope and testing.

How are Orbit and Vista different from a typical gateway or interface?

Orbit connects and coordinates business capabilities from existing systems, while Vista brings those capabilities to users in one consistent workspace. Compatibility for each system is assessed before adoption.

What does starting with one insurance business function mean?

Choose one specific function, agree the current state, acceptance criteria and connections to preserve. The assessment result is the basis for deciding whether to expand or verify further.

What happens when a transaction outcome is unclear?

The system stops and reports clearly; it does not replay the transaction automatically. Operations reconcile through the agreed process. A timeout does not mean the transaction was rolled back.

Does Vista replace the current interface immediately?

Not necessarily. Start with one group of functions and run it alongside the current interface.

What is needed to deploy?

Define the insurance business function to assess, review compatibility with the existing system, and agree on goals, acceptance criteria and the specific deployment conditions. These conditions are confirmed before work begins.

Where does Nexus fit in the model?

Nexus is one of NOVA’s four solution families. Current tools concentrate on build and shared administration; the DevOps, batch and daily operations scope is confirmed against specific needs.

What is Aster?

Aster is demonstration software that ships with the Orbit family, so a working flow can be experienced without using a running system. Evaluating a real system still requires compatibility review and an agreed test scope.
